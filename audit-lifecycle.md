# Audit Lifecycle Overview

## Purpose
This document describes the standard IT audit lifecycle and how audit activities
repeat over time. It is intended as a learning reference and to illustrate how
individual audit artifacts fit into a continuous audit process.

---

## 1. Planning & Scoping
Audits begin by defining scope based on risk, regulatory requirements, and audit
objectives.

Key activities:
- Identify audit objectives
- Define in-scope and out-of-scope areas
- Prioritize high-risk domains (e.g., access, privileged roles, SoD)
- Identify applicable policies, standards, and controls

Outputs:
- Audit scope documentation
- Control inventory
- Risk-based prioritization

---

## 2. Control Identification
Controls relevant to the audit scope are identified and mapped to policies,
standards, or regulatory requirements.

Examples:
- Logical access controls
- Change management controls
- Backup and recovery controls
- Business continuity controls

Outputs:
- Control listings
- Control descriptions
- Control owners

---

## 3. Evidence Collection
Evidence is requested from control owners or system owners to support control
design and operation.

Common evidence types:
- System reports and exports
- Logs and configuration settings
- Access review or certification records
- Tickets and approval records
- Policies and procedures

Audit principle:
Evidence must be sufficient, relevant, and reliable.

---

## 4. Sampling & Testing
Due to large populations, auditors use sampling to test control effectiveness.

Activities:
- Select samples using risk-based judgment
- Perform test procedures
- Compare actual results to expected control criteria
- Document results in workpapers

Outputs:
- Completed test steps
- Pass/fail determinations
- Identified exceptions

---

## 5. Exception Evaluation
When a control does not operate as intended, an exception is documented.

Activities:
- Describe condition and criteria
- Assess potential risk or impact
- Determine severity (low/medium/high)
- Validate understanding with control owners

Important note:
Auditors document and report exceptions but do not remediate them.

---

## 6. Reporting & Communication
Audit results are communicated to management and relevant stakeholders.

Activities:
- Summarize testing results
- Document exceptions and observations
- Capture management responses
- Maintain professional, objective communication

Outputs:
- Audit reports
- Exception summaries
- Management response documentation

---

## 7. Remediation & Follow-Up
Control owners are responsible for remediation of identified issues.

Auditor role:
- Track remediation status
- Validate corrective actions in future audits
- Re-test controls in subsequent cycles

Audit independence is maintained throughout this process.

---

## 8. Re-Testing & Continuous Cycle
Audits are iterative and recurring.

In future audit cycles:
- Previously tested controls may be re-tested
- Scope may change based on risk trends
- Persistent issues receive increased attention

This continuous cycle supports governance, risk management, and compliance
objectives over time.

---

## Learning Note
This repository represents a simulated audit cycle for educational and skill-
building purposes. In practice, audit cycles vary by organization, risk profile,
and regulatory environment.
