# Business Continuity & Disaster Recovery (BCP/DR) – Audit Example

## Control Objective
Ensure critical systems have documented business continuity and disaster recovery plans that are periodically tested and reviewed to support system availability during disruptions.

---

## Control Description
Management maintains business continuity and disaster recovery plans for critical systems. Plans are reviewed periodically and tested according to policy to ensure recoverability objectives are met.

---

## Audit Scope
This review focuses on:
- Existence of documented BCP/DR plans
- Evidence of periodic testing
- Management review and approval
- Alignment to policy requirements

This audit does not assess the technical execution of recovery procedures.

---

## Population
Critical systems identified by management:
- ERP
- HRIS
- Active Directory

Population size: 3 systems

---

## Sampling Approach
Risk-based sampling:
- All systems in scope were reviewed due to high business impact

---

## Evidence Reviewed
- Business continuity plans
- Disaster recovery test results
- Management approval records
- Test schedules and completion dates

---

## Test Steps
For each system:
1. Verify a documented BCP/DR plan exists
2. Confirm plan includes recovery objectives
3. Verify testing occurred within required frequency
4. Confirm management review and approval
5. Validate test evidence supports completion

---

## Results Summary
- ERP: Pass (plan exists; test performed; approval documented)
- HRIS: Exception (test not performed within required timeframe)
- Active Directory: Pass (plan and test evidence complete)

---

## Exception Detail (Mock)
System: HRIS  
Condition: Disaster recovery test not performed within policy-defined frequency  
Criteria: BCP/DR testing must occur per policy schedule  
Impact: Reduced assurance of system recoverability during disruption  
Severity: Medium  

Audit Action:
- Exception documented
- Escalated to management for response
- No remediation performed by audit

---

## Conclusion
Controls related to business continuity and disaster recovery are generally operating effectively, with one exception noted for management action.
